## Why This Section Matters

"This screen feels overwhelming" is a real observation, but it is useless as a design instruction, because it does not say what to cut. Cognitive load theory gives you the diagnostic vocabulary that turns "overwhelming" into a plan: some difficulty is intrinsic to the material, some is extraneous noise the design added, and some is the productive germane effort that actually builds understanding. The whole point of a load analysis is that the three demand the opposite of each other; you manage intrinsic load, eliminate extraneous load, and protect germane load. Cutting the wrong one (deleting practice to "reduce load") destroys learning.

This section teaches you to diagnose a draft screen by load type rather than by a single overall rating. You should leave able to attribute each piece of demand to a specific screen feature and prescribe the matching move: stage the intrinsic, strip the extraneous, keep the germane.

## Learning Targets

- Classify a screen's demand into intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load with evidence.
- Attribute each load source to a specific feature, not a vague overall feeling.
- Prescribe the type-appropriate move (segment, remove, protect) for each source.
- Recognize when a "reduce load" suggestion would actually cut germane processing.

## Opening Case

A pilot tester stalls for two minutes on screen 3, the introduction to standard deviation. The draft margin note reads "High, should reduce: delete the practice set." Deleting practice would indeed make the screen feel lighter, and it would also remove the only part of the screen where a schema gets built. The stall is real, but its cause is element interactivity (mean, deviation, squaring, and averaging all introduced at once), which is intrinsic load to be staged, not germane load to be cut. The right move lowers demand and keeps the learning; the proposed move lowers demand by deleting the learning.

The case is the central trap of load analysis: every kind of load makes a screen feel heavier, so a single "feels hard" rating cannot tell you what to do. Only a typed diagnosis distinguishes the difficulty worth keeping from the noise worth removing.

## Core Concept

Cognitive load theory partitions the demand on working memory into three types. Intrinsic load is set by the complexity of the material itself, specifically its element interactivity: how many elements the learner must hold and relate simultaneously. You cannot delete intrinsic load without changing the objective, but you can stage it through segmenting, pre-training, and worked examples so it arrives in processable pieces. Extraneous load is demand created by the design rather than the content: redundant on-screen text that competes with narration, split-attention layouts that force the eye to integrate separated sources, decorative seductive details. Extraneous load has no instructional value, so the move is always to remove it. Germane load is the effort the learner spends building and automating schemas, exactly the productive work that practice and self-explanation create. The move for germane load is to protect it.

The diagnostic discipline is attribution: every load entry must point at a specific feature and a load type, because the prescription depends entirely on the type. The same surface symptom (a hard screen) routes to opposite actions.

| Load type | Source on the screen | Right move | Wrong move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intrinsic | Four interacting concepts introduced at once | Segment; pre-train the mean first | Leave unstaged and hope |
| Extraneous | On-screen paragraph duplicating the narration | Remove the redundant text | Reformat it to look nicer |
| Germane | Two practice items after the worked steps | Keep; this builds the schema | Delete to "reduce load" |

The theory matters because total working-memory capacity is fixed and shared. Extraneous load steals capacity that germane processing needs; removing extraneous noise is not just tidying, it frees capacity for the learning to happen. That is why the order of operations is strip extraneous first, then stage intrinsic, then verify germane survived.

Model the additive load equation against a fixed capacity. Total load is the sum of the three types, \$L = I + E + G\$, and headroom is whatever capacity remains after that sum, \$H = C - (I + E + G)\$. When headroom goes negative the learner stalls. ## Worked Example

Work through one annotated before/after revision of a cognitive load diagnosis table for a single draft screen.

**Before (diagnosis table, screen 3):**

| Screen | Load rating |
| --- | --- |
| 3 (intro to standard deviation) | High, should reduce |

**Annotations a reviewer would leave:**

1. "High" is one undifferentiated rating. It does not say whether the difficulty is intrinsic (the statistics itself) or extraneous (something the design added).
2. "Should reduce" gives no target and no mechanism, so any later revision is untestable.
3. The proposed fix in the margin, "delete the practice set," would cut germane load, which is the effort that actually builds the schema.

**After (revised diagnosis table, screen 3):**

| Screen | Source of demand | Load type | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | High element interactivity: mean, deviation, squaring, and averaging introduced at once | Intrinsic | Segment into 3 worked steps with pre-training on the mean before the full formula |
| 3 | Definition paragraph on screen while narration reads different wording | Extraneous | Remove the redundant on-screen paragraph; keep narration plus the labeled diagram |
| 3 | Two practice items after the worked steps | Germane | Keep; this is the productive effort and should not be cut |

**Why the after version is stronger:** it separates the three load types, attributes each to a specific screen feature, and prescribes the right move for each (stage intrinsic, remove extraneous, protect germane). A reviewer can now test whether segmenting and removing the redundant text lowered total demand without removing the learning. The change is a diagnosis, not a one-word rating.

## Decision Rule

Never act on an overall load rating; act on a typed, attributed source. For each entry in the diagnosis table:

1. **Type check:** Is this demand intrinsic, extraneous, or germane, with a reason?
2. **Attribution check:** Does it point at a specific feature, not the whole screen?
3. **Move check:** Does the action match the type (stage intrinsic, remove extraneous, protect germane), and would it cut any learning?

If a proposed reduction would lower germane load, reject it; you are about to delete the learning to make the screen feel easier.

## Common Misreadings

The classic error is conflating difficulty with bad design and trying to remove all of it; some difficulty (germane effort) is the entire point. The mirror error is rating a screen "high" without typing the load, which produces actions that lower demand by gutting practice. A third error is treating extraneous load as cosmetic; redundant on-screen text is not just ugly, it actively competes for the capacity germane processing needs.

Difficulty is not the enemy; undifferentiated difficulty is, because it hides which demand to cut and which to keep.

## Textbook Walkthrough

Walk a screen feature by feature and ask of each one: is this demand coming from the content, from the design, or from the learner's own effort to understand? Content-side demand is intrinsic, design-side demand is extraneous, effort-side demand is germane. Naming the source forces the type, and the type forces the move.

Then sequence the moves correctly. Strip the extraneous first, because freeing that capacity sometimes resolves a stall on its own and changes how much staging the intrinsic load needs. Stage the intrinsic next through segmenting and pre-training. Verify last that the germane work (practice, self-explanation) is still present and was not collateral damage.

Finally, make each move testable. "Segment into 3 worked steps" and "remove the redundant paragraph" are checkable against a re-test of the same stall; "reduce load" is not. A load analysis is only as good as the re-measurement it sets up.

## What Strong Reading Looks Like

As you read, keep a two-column note: **claim** on the left, **evidence** on the right. The claim is the load type you assign a feature; the evidence is the specific reason (element interactivity, redundancy, schema-building) that justifies the type. By the end of AIL 606 04.04, your notes should let a reviewer check that no proposed cut would remove germane processing.

A strong reader of a load diagnosis can answer four questions:

1. For each demand source, what is the load type and why?
2. Which feature creates the extraneous load, and what is the removal move?
3. Where is the germane processing, and how is it protected?
4. Which seemingly helpful cut would actually delete learning?

## Stronger Artifact Standard

The artifact is strong when every entry names a source, a type, and a type-matched move, and when no action lowers germane load. Treat a single overall rating as the weakest possible diagnosis. Where you are unsure whether demand is intrinsic or extraneous, mark it with the annotation tools and ask BigAL to contrast an intrinsic-load fix (segmenting) with an extraneous-load fix (removing redundancy) rather than for a generic definition of load.

A useful final paragraph for the artifact would sound like this: "I segmented the standard-deviation intro to stage its intrinsic load and removed the redundant on-screen paragraph to cut extraneous load. I rejected the suggestion to delete the practice items, because that demand is germane and is where the schema actually forms."

## Transfer Case

Move the same diagnosis from novice learners to advanced learners who already hold the prerequisite schemas. Now the redundancy and worked-example supports that helped novices can backfire through the [expertise-reversal effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expertise_reversal_effect): for experts, the extra scaffolding becomes extraneous load. The element interactivity that was intrinsic for novices is largely automated for experts, so what was germane staging is now redundant. Transfer means re-typing the same features for a different prior-knowledge level, not reusing the novice table. If you understand load theory, raising expertise flips some supports from helpful to harmful.
