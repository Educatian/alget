# Blackboard-to-ALGET Alignment

![Blackboard-to-ALGET Alignment textbook visual](/course-art/deep-exemplars/ail606-supplement/08-07-blackboard-to-alget-alignment.png)

## Why This Section Matters

Migrating a course from one platform to another tempts you to treat it as a transfer problem: copy the modules, repoint the links, done. But platforms differ in what they can capture, and a faithful copy of the wrong structure carries the original's misalignments into the new system intact. Moving from Blackboard to ALGET is an opportunity to ask the question the original course may have skipped: does every learning objective actually have an activity that develops it and an assessment that measures it?

This matters because ALGET's adaptive features depend on process evidence (the trace of how a learner reasoned), and a Blackboard dropbox captures only a finished file. A lift-and-shift migration hands ALGET a product with no process to read, disabling the very feature you migrated for. Alignment work is what turns a platform change into a course improvement rather than a more expensive way to deliver the same gaps.

## Opening Case

A coordinator migrates a design module from Blackboard to ALGET over a weekend by exporting the course package and importing it whole. Everything appears: the Module 8 quiz, the storyboard dropbox, the gradebook columns. It looks like a clean move. Then the team maps the imported activities to the four stated objectives and finds the truth the export hid: all ten quiz items measure Objective 1 (recall of definitions), Objectives 2 through 4 have no activity at all, and the dropbox captures finished files that ALGET's adaptive support cannot read because there is no process trace.

The case shows that a successful import is not a successful migration. The package transferred faithfully; the alignment never existed to transfer. As you read, treat migration as the moment to build the objective-to-activity-to-assessment map the original course was missing, not as a chance to reproduce its structure faster.

## Core Concept

Blackboard-to-ALGET alignment applies constructive alignment, Biggs's principle that learning objectives, learning activities, and assessments must all target the same intended verbs, to a platform migration. In a well-aligned course, if an objective says "analyze a defensible decision," some activity has students analyzing decisions and some assessment captures that analysis. Misalignment is the silent default: courses accumulate quizzes and dropboxes that measure whatever was easy to build, not what the objectives promised.

Three ideas make alignment operational during a migration:

- **The alignment table is the diagnosis.** A two-column table (objective, then the activity and assessment that target it) exposes empty cells. The lift-and-shift left three of four cells empty; "we moved everything" is an inventory, not an alignment.
- **Match the response format to the cognitive verb.** A multiple-choice item can test recall (Objective 1) but cannot capture analysis or a documented AI-boundary decision. Forcing a higher-order objective into a recall format measures the wrong construct and adds load on a skill the item was never meant to assess.
- **Migrate for the new platform's affordances.** ALGET reads process traces, so a storyboard dropbox should become an artifact-studio before-and-after trace that captures the decision and the revision, feeding the adaptive support that motivated the migration.

| Objective verb | Blackboard activity (lifted) | Aligned ALGET activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recall definitions | 10 MC quiz items | One or two MC items retained |
| Analyze a defensible decision | none | Artifact-studio before/after trace |
| Document a bounded AI move | none | Misconception-tagged practice on the AI boundary |
| Decide next revision | none | Trace plus adaptive-support prompt |

## Worked Example

Walk through one concrete alignment decision: moving a design module's assessments from Blackboard into ALGET without losing the link between learning objectives and what each activity measures.

**Before (the lift-and-shift copy).** The first migration copied the Blackboard structure verbatim: a "Module 8 Quiz" (10 auto-graded multiple-choice items) and a "Storyboard Dropbox" (single upload, instructor grades later). Mapped against the four learning objectives, two problems surfaced. The 10 quiz items all measured Objective 1 (recall of definitions); Objectives 2 through 4 (analyze a defensible decision, document a bounded AI move, decide next revision) had no aligned activity at all. And the dropbox captured a finished file but no process evidence, so ALGET's adaptive support had nothing to read.

1. **Build the alignment table first, not the activities.** List each objective in one column and the activity that measures it in the next. The lift-and-shift left three cells empty. Alignment is the diagnosis; "we moved everything" is not.
2. **Map each ALGET feature to the objective it serves.** The storyboard upload becomes an artifact-studio before/after trace (serves Objective 2 and 4, because it captures the decision and the revision). One quiz item is rewritten as a misconception-tagged practice item that probes Objective 3 (the AI-boundary decision). By **[cognitive load theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_load)**, matching the response format to the objective keeps assessment load on the target skill rather than on recall it was not meant to test.
3. **Confirm coverage before going live.** Re-check the table: every objective now has at least one aligned ALGET activity, and no activity is orphaned from an objective.

**After (the course as an aligned migration).** The alignment table moved from 1 of 4 objectives covered to 4 of 4, with the dropbox replaced by a trace that ALGET's adaptive support can actually read. The change is auditable: a reviewer can compare the lift-and-shift inventory to the objective-to-activity table and see which empty cells were filled and how.

## Decision Rule

Build the alignment table before you build or migrate a single activity. The table is the diagnosis; activities chosen without it inherit whatever the old platform happened to contain. Migrate toward the new platform's strengths, not toward the old platform's habits.

Run any migration plan through three checks:

1. **Coverage check:** Does every objective have at least one activity that develops it and one assessment that measures it, with no empty cells? A lifted course usually fails this on its higher-order objectives.
2. **Verb-format match check:** Does each assessment's response format fit the objective's cognitive verb? A recall item cannot stand in for an analysis objective, no matter how cleanly it imported.
3. **Affordance check:** Does the migrated activity use what the new platform can do that the old one could not? If a dropbox stays a dropbox, ALGET's process-reading features have nothing to read, and the migration bought nothing.

A plan that fails these moved the course without improving it, and often disabled the features that justified moving.

## Common Misreadings

The most common misread is that a complete import equals a complete migration. The export tool copies whatever exists, including the gaps; a flawless transfer of a misaligned course is a flawless transfer of misalignment. Fidelity to the source is not a virtue when the source was never aligned.

A second misread is that adding the new platform's features is the same as using them. Replacing a dropbox with an artifact-studio block only helps if the activity actually elicits a process trace; an artifact-studio that students treat as a second dropbox feeds ALGET the same dead-end product. The affordance has to be wired to a task that produces what it can read.

## Textbook Walkthrough

Re-align the lifted module step by step. Start with the table, not the activities: list the four objectives down one column. The import filled only the recall row; three rows are empty, which is the real finding the clean import concealed.

Now fill the cells by matching format to verb. Objective 2 (analyze a defensible decision) and Objective 4 (decide next revision) both need captured reasoning, so the storyboard dropbox becomes an artifact-studio before-and-after trace that records the decision and the revision. Objective 3 (document a bounded AI move) needs a probe of judgment, so one quiz item is rewritten as a misconception-tagged practice item on the AI boundary. Objective 1 keeps a couple of the recall items, which were never the problem.

Finally, re-check the table for coverage and orphans: every objective now maps to an aligned activity, and no activity floats unattached to an objective. The walkthrough is complete when the migration has moved from one of four objectives covered to four of four, and the dropbox has become a trace ALGET's adaptive support can actually read, which is the whole reason the course moved platforms.

## What Strong Reading Looks Like

A strong reader of this section instinctively builds the alignment table when handed any course, looking first for empty cells where an objective has no activity or assessment. Constructive alignment is the lens; the empty cell is the defect it reveals.

Read any migrated or inherited course against these four questions:

1. Does every objective map to an activity that develops it and an assessment that measures it?
2. Does each assessment's format match the cognitive verb of its objective, or does a recall item stand in for higher-order work?
3. Does the migration exploit the new platform's affordances, or merely reproduce the old structure?
4. Is any activity orphaned, present in the course but attached to no objective?

## Stronger Artifact Standard

A strong alignment artifact shows the before table (objectives versus lifted activities, with empty cells visible) and the after table (every cell filled with a format-matched activity), plus a note on which dropbox became a process-capturing trace. A reviewer should be able to see coverage move from one of four objectives to four of four and to see why each new activity fits its objective's verb.

A strong rationale sounds like this: "I converted the storyboard dropbox into an artifact-studio trace because Objectives 2 and 4 require captured reasoning that a finished-file upload cannot show, and ALGET's adaptive support needs a process trace to read. I rejected the suggestion to keep all ten quiz items, because nine of them measured recall that only Objective 1 called for and left the higher-order objectives unassessed."

## Transfer Case

Move the same alignment work to a migration into a platform that, unlike ALGET, cannot capture process traces and supports only quizzes and file uploads. Constructive alignment is unchanged, but the affordance constraint inverts: you cannot rely on a process-reading feature to assess the analysis objectives. The faithful transfer is to meet the higher-order objectives with what the platform can do, perhaps a structured upload whose template forces a documented before-and-after rationale, and to accept heavier instructor reading where automated reading is impossible. Copying the ALGET artifact-studio expectation into a platform that cannot read traces would leave the analysis objectives nominally covered but actually unassessable.
