## Why This Section Matters

A checklist that says "all six files submitted" confirms assembly, not completion. The six capstone components, the iteration log, the AI-USE.md, the quality review, the theoretical defense, the peer responses, and the slides, were each produced at a different time for a different purpose, and nothing guarantees they tell the same story. A completion studio is the final pass that treats the package as one argument and checks it for internal coherence: do the claims in your slides match the data in your log, does your theoretical defense reference fixes your quality review demanded, does your AI-USE.md agree with the rationale in your matrix. Contradictions across components are the single most common thing a committee catches, because each document was checked alone and never against the others.

The deeper purpose is synthesis. A capstone is not six deliverables; it is evidence of a designer's growth, and the completion studio is where you make that growth legible, naming how your judgment changed from the first iteration entry to the last. That reflective synthesis is what turns a folder of files into a defensible body of work.

## Learning Targets

- Treat a six-component capstone package as one argument and list every cross-component claim it makes.
- Reconcile contradictory claims by correcting the inaccurate component toward the recorded evidence, not by inflating the log.
- Verify any AI-assembled package summary against each underlying artifact before submitting.
- Write a reflective synthesis naming how your design judgment changed across the module.

## Opening Case

A student submits and writes: "Iteration log, AI-USE.md, quality review, theoretical defense, peer responses, slides. All six files complete." Every file exists, so the student believes the capstone is done. But the slides claim "five rounds of learner testing" while the iteration log records two tryouts, and the theoretical defense praises the segmenting on screen 3 while the quality review flagged screen 3's missing captions as a blocker. Each file was internally fine; together they contradict, and a committee that reads them side by side will find the seams the student never checked.

The completion studio catches this before the committee does. It reads the package as one document, reconciles the numbers and claims that disagree, and adds a synthesis of how the work changed, so the capstone reads as coherent evidence rather than six independently produced files stapled together.

## Core Concept

The completion studio performs two operations that a submission checklist cannot: **cross-artifact coherence checking** and **reflective synthesis**.

Coherence checking treats every quantitative and qualitative claim as something that must agree with the same claim wherever else it appears. The method is a reconciliation pass: for each component, list its load-bearing claims, then find every other component that touches the same claim and check for agreement.

| Claim source | Claim to verify | Cross-check against | Resolution if they disagree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slides | Testing counts, completion rates | Iteration log | Correct the slide to match the recorded data |
| Theoretical defense | A principle applied to a screen | Quality review of that screen | Reconcile so the defense and the review tell one story |
| AI-USE.md | What a tool produced and you kept | Iteration log rationale | Confirm the same decision appears in both, or fix the gap |

A reconciliation pass means knowing which component to cross-check each claim against. Match each claim source to the component that can confirm or contradict it.

The principle is that completion means coherence, not assembly. A package where the slides and the log disagree on how many tryouts you ran is not complete at any number of files, because the contradiction is itself a defect the committee will treat as carelessness or, worse, as inflation.

Reflective synthesis is the second operation and the one that distinguishes a capstone from a course assignment. It is a metacognitive account of how your design judgment changed across the module: what you documented in the first iteration entry (often raw version counts) versus the last (problem-change-evidence chains with rejected alternatives), and what you would do differently next time (log AI use in real time rather than reconstruct it). This is the demonstration that you internalized the practice, not just performed it, which is the actual outcome a capstone certifies.

## Decision Rule

Before you call the capstone done, run the package as one document, not six. Existence of files is the lowest bar; agreement among them is the real one.

1. **Coherence test:** Does every number and claim that appears in more than one component agree across all of them?
2. **Reconciliation test:** Where components disagreed, did you correct the inaccurate one rather than leave both standing?
3. **Synthesis test:** Have you named how your design judgment changed across the module and what you would do differently?

If any test fails, the package is assembled but not complete. Reconcile the contradictions and write the synthesis before submitting.

## Common Misreadings

The most common error is equating a full file list with a finished capstone. Submission and completion are different states: a submitted package can contain contradictions that make it indefensible, while a complete package has been reconciled into one consistent account. The checklist confirms the first; only the coherence pass confirms the second.

A second error is omitting the reflective synthesis because the artifacts "speak for themselves." They do not speak to your growth; they show snapshots, and only the synthesis connects them into a trajectory. A committee reading a capstone wants to see that the designer changed, and the synthesis is the only component that makes that change explicit rather than leaving it to be inferred.

## Textbook Walkthrough

Lay all six components side by side and make a single list of every claim that appears in more than one of them: testing counts, completion numbers, which principle governs which screen, which AI output you kept. For each shared claim, check that the components agree, and when they do not, decide which one is correct and fix the others to match it. This is tedious and it is exactly where committees find the inflation and carelessness that weaken otherwise strong work.

Then write the synthesis. Reread your first iteration entry and your last, and describe in plain language what changed in how you documented decisions, what you now know that you did not at the start, and what you would change in your process next time. The walkthrough ends with a package whose claims agree everywhere they appear and a synthesis that makes your growth as a designer the explicit conclusion of the capstone.

## What Strong Reading Looks Like

As you read, pull the one number that appears in the most components of your own package and verify it agrees in all of them. Strong reading here means assuming a contradiction exists until you have checked, because the contradictions that sink capstones are precisely the ones the author was confident were not there.

A strong reader of a completion studio can do four things:

1. Treat the package as one argument and list every cross-component claim.
2. Reconcile disagreements by correcting the inaccurate component.
3. Distinguish submission (files exist) from completion (files agree).
4. Write a reflective synthesis that makes design growth explicit.

## Stronger Artifact Standard

A strong completed capstone is judged by whether a committee reading all six components finds one consistent story and a clear account of growth. That requires a coherence pass that reconciled every shared claim and a synthesis that names what changed in the designer's judgment. The standard fails when the package is merely assembled, because internal contradictions read as either carelessness or inflated claims, both of which a defense will expose. When you are unsure whether two components agree, annotate the shared claim and request a comparison between a reconciled package and an assembled one.

A final paragraph for the artifact would read like this: "I read the six components as one document, reconciled every claim that appeared in more than one, and corrected the slides to match the log where they disagreed. I closed with a synthesis of how my documentation moved from version counts to evidence chains, which is the growth this capstone is meant to demonstrate."

## Transfer Case

Move the completion logic to a team deliverable, where the six components were authored by different people rather than one designer over time. The coherence problem intensifies: now contradictions arise not from your own forgetfulness but from teammates working with different numbers and assumptions, so the reconciliation pass becomes a cross-author audit before delivery. The reflective synthesis shifts too, from individual growth to a shared account of how the team's design decisions converged. The transferable skill is producing one coherent argument from independently authored parts, which is the condition almost every professional design deliverable, from an RFP response to a launch package, actually arrives in.
