The seven prior sections each taught a strong move in isolation: frames as claims, signaling and segmenting, image commissioning, modality, accessibility, prompt logs, and peer review. The studio is where they have to work together, and integration is its own skill, distinct from any of the parts. A storyboard that staples seven good techniques onto separate frames is not a designed sequence; it is a portfolio of demonstrations. The capstone failure is not a weak frame but an incoherent whole, individually polished frames that do not build toward one objective and that apply principles inconsistently across the sequence.

This section treats the studio as an exercise in coherence across frames plus an accountable decision record. Coherence means every frame traces to one objective and the prior-state-to-action chain actually links frame to frame, so a principle honored on Frame 2 is not abandoned on Frame 4. The decision record means the integrated storyboard ships with a short rationale, key choices, rejected options, and warrants, so a reviewer can audit the sequence as a single argument rather than admiring frames one at a time.

## Learning Targets

- Analyze how the storyboard frame sequence with narration, signaling, and reader-action notes shows a defensible decision in AIL 606.
- Distinguish artifact polish from evidence-based revision.
- Document a bounded AI/software support move with accepted and rejected suggestions.
- Use annotations, support requests, and revision notes to decide what help or design change should come next.

## Opening Case

A team assembles its capstone storyboard the night before it is due. Frame 1 reuses the polished claim from 03.01; Frame 3 drops in the verified lever diagram from 03.03 with its alt text from 03.05 pasted underneath; the peer comments from 03.07 are stapled to the end. Each piece is genuinely good. But Frame 2 gives the learner a Continue control while Frame 4 auto-advances through three dense steps, the segmenting principle honored in one place and broken in another, and no thread connects the frames toward a single objective.

A reviewer reading frame by frame would praise each panel. A reviewer reading the sequence sees a stapled collection with an internal contradiction. The strong frames hide the weak whole. This section asks you to expose the seam: the studio is graded on whether the sequence is one coherent argument toward an objective, not on whether each frame is individually impressive.

## Core Concept

Integration rests on two ideas that do not appear when you work one frame at a time.

The first is **sequence coherence**. A storyboard is coherent when every frame serves one objective and the chain of claims actually links: the predicted action of Frame N becomes the prior state of Frame N+1 (the discipline from 03.01 applied across the whole sequence). Incoherence shows up as a gap (two frames with no connecting step), a redundancy (two frames making the same move), or an inconsistency (a principle applied here and abandoned there). Coherence is checked by reading the sequence as a path, not by inspecting frames in isolation.

The second is the **decision record** as the unit of evidence. At capstone scale, no reviewer can re-derive every choice from the frames alone, so the storyboard ships with a one-page record: the major decisions, the options rejected, and the warrant for each. The record is what makes a long artifact auditable, and it is where the seven prior skills become legible as deliberate choices rather than lucky defaults.

The course anchors now function as a consistency audit across the whole. Mayer's principles must be applied the same way in comparable frames (segmenting on Frame 2 implies segmenting on Frame 4). Cognitive load theory governs the accumulation of load across the sequence, not just within a frame, because a learner's working memory carries over. UDL learner variability is checked end to end: the named target learner must be able to traverse the entire path, not merely succeed on the easiest frame. A principle that holds frame by frame but contradicts itself across frames signals that the parts were assembled, not integrated.

## Worked Example: Integrating a Module-3 Storyboard

The studio task is a five-frame storyboard teaching one objective: the learner can predict the next stage of the water cycle. Below is the before/after of the capstone as a whole.

**Before (a collection of strong but disconnected pieces):**

> Frame 1 reuses the polished claim from 03.01. Frame 3 drops in the verified lever-style diagram skill from 03.03. The alt text from 03.05 is pasted under it. The peer comments from 03.07 are attached at the end. No connecting rationale. Frame 2 has a Continue control; Frame 4 auto-advances through three dense steps.

Problems: each piece is good in isolation, but the sequence has an inconsistency (segmenting honored on Frame 2, broken on Frame 4) and reads as a stapled collection, not an integrated storyboard with one objective.

**After (integrated toward the objective, with a decision record):**

> All five frames trace one objective: setup (name the stages), claim frames (predict the next stage), evidence frame (check the prediction). Segmenting is applied consistently: every frame ends on a learner-paced Continue. Modality, image, and alt-text choices each cite the principle and the objective they serve. A one-page decision record lists the major choices, the rejected options, and the warrant for each.

Annotated decision trail:

1. Traced the objective across all five frames and closed the gap so the sequence builds to the prediction, instead of optimizing frames in isolation.
2. Made segmenting consistent: fixed Frame 4 to match Frame 2's learner pacing.
3. Integrated, did not collect: each reused skill (claim, image, alt text, review) is justified by how it serves this one objective.
4. Attached the decision record: key choices, rejected options (for example, an AI-suggested sixth bonus frame, rejected for diluting the objective), and the principle or learner reasoning behind each.

The improvement is one coherent storyboard with a visible evidence trail, not a folder of strong but unconnected artifacts.

## Decision Rule

Audit the storyboard as a whole before judging any frame, using the integration sequence:

1. **Objective trace:** Walk every frame and ask "how does this serve the one objective?" A frame that serves no objective, or a second objective, is the seam to fix; cut it or fold it in.
2. **Chain check:** Confirm Frame N's predicted action is Frame N+1's prior state. Gaps and redundancies both break the chain and both need a revision.
3. **Consistency sweep:** Pick one principle (segmenting, modality, accessibility) and check it is applied the same way in every comparable frame. Inconsistency is the signature of assembly, not integration.
4. **Record check:** Does a one-page decision record list the major choices, the rejected options, and a warrant for each? If a reviewer would have to ask you, the record is incomplete.

A capstone passes when the sequence reads as one argument and the record lets a reviewer audit it without you in the room.

## Common Misreadings

The first misreading is that a strong capstone is the sum of strong frames. It is not; a sequence of excellent but disconnected frames fails on coherence, which is graded at the level of the whole. The second misreading is that the decision record is paperwork added after the design. At capstone scale the record is part of the design, because it is the only thing that makes a multi-frame artifact auditable; a storyboard whose rationale lives only in the authors' heads cannot be reviewed or revised by anyone else, including a teammate next week.

## Textbook Walkthrough

Walk the stapled water-cycle capstone toward integration. The objective is "the learner can predict the next stage of the water cycle." Run the objective trace: Frames 1 and 3 serve it, but an AI tool suggested a sixth bonus frame on cloud types that does not, so it is cut for diluting the objective. Run the chain check: Frame 1 names the stages (prior state), the claim frames have the learner predict the next stage (action), and an evidence frame checks the prediction, so the chain links once Frame 5's stray content is removed. Run the consistency sweep on segmenting: Frame 2 ends on a learner Continue but Frame 4 auto-advances, so Frame 4 is fixed to match, removing the contradiction. Finally, attach a decision record listing the cut bonus frame, the segmenting fix, and the warrant for each reused skill.

The result is not seven trophies on a shelf; it is one coherent storyboard that builds to a prediction, applies its principles consistently, and ships with a record a reviewer can audit. The walkthrough shows integration is mostly subtraction and alignment, cutting what does not serve the objective and making comparable frames behave alike, not adding more.

## What Strong Reading Looks Like

Read the capstone as a path, not a gallery. Trace the objective through every frame and flag the first frame that serves a different goal; follow the prior-state-to-action chain and flag the first broken link; pick one principle and flag the first inconsistency. A strong reader resists being charmed by an individually beautiful frame and instead asks whether it earns its place in the sequence. Read the decision record adversarially: for each major choice, ask whether the stated warrant would actually survive a reviewer's challenge.

A strong reader of an integrated storyboard can name its single objective, point to any broken link or inconsistency in the chain, and judge whether the decision record makes the whole auditable without the author.

## Stronger Artifact Standard

The artifact reaches the stronger standard when the sequence is coherent end to end and ships with an auditable decision record. Every frame traces to one objective, the claim chain links without gaps or redundancies, at least one principle is verifiably applied the same way across comparable frames, and the record lists major choices, rejected options (including at least one tool suggestion declined for diluting the objective), and a warrant each. Reused skills from earlier sections appear as justified choices, not pasted fragments.

A strong closing note reads: "I cut the AI-suggested bonus frame because it served cloud types, not the prediction objective. I fixed Frame 4 to end on a learner Continue so segmenting is consistent with Frame 2. Each reused skill, the claim frame, the verified image, the alt text, the peer-review record, is justified by how it serves the one objective, and the attached decision record lets a reviewer audit the sequence without asking me to narrate it."

## Transfer Case

Move the integration discipline from a single self-paced module to a multi-week course where this storyboard is one unit among several, built by rotating team members. Coherence now operates at two scales: within the storyboard and across units that must not contradict each other or repeat the same move, and the decision record becomes a handoff document so the next team can extend the design without re-deriving it. The transfer test is whether you can keep the objective-trace and consistency-sweep discipline when the "whole" is a course, not a module, and when the auditor is a future teammate rather than an instructor. If your integration skill only works on artifacts you built alone and finished in one sitting, it does not scale; the durable skill is making coherence and rationale legible to people who arrive after you.
