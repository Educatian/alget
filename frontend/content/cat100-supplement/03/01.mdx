## Core Concept

The core idea in this section is that **Spreadsheet Structure and Clean Data** should be treated as a relationship among purpose, constraint, evidence, and revision. Purpose names what the learner or audience needs to accomplish. Constraint names what limits the decision. Evidence names the source that makes the decision defensible. Revision shows what changed after the evidence was considered.

Three course anchors shape the reasoning here:

- **NACE career readiness** helps you decide what counts as a meaningful learning or performance demand.
- **ISTE digital citizenship** helps you notice when a design or technology choice creates risk, burden, or unsupported assumptions.
- **data-ink and visual evidence** helps you check whether the artifact works for varied learners, audiences, or use contexts.

The anchors should do work in your artifact. If NACE career readiness or ISTE digital citizenship does not change a decision, it is only a citation rather than evidence of judgment.

## Worked Example

Suppose a downloaded export arrives looking like this, with a banner title, a blank row, mixed name format, and inconsistent regions:

```
   A              B        C
1  Q3 Sales Report
2
3  Name           Region   Units
4  Lopez, Maria   TX       18
5  Chen, David    texas     9
6  Park, Sun      Tx       12
```

Step 1. Delete rows 1 and 2 so the header row `Name | Region | Units` becomes row 1 with no banner or blank row above it.

Step 2. Split column A on the comma with Text to Columns so `Lopez, Maria` becomes `Lopez` in one column and `Maria` in the next. Now one value lives in one cell.

Step 3. Standardize the Region column. Replace `texas` and `Tx` with `TX` so all three rows read `TX`. A pivot will now count one region, not three.

Step 4. Confirm Units is numeric. `=SUM(units)` returns `39`. If any cell had been typed as `18 units`, that text cell would have been skipped and the total would read `21`, which is the visible symptom of the numbers-stored-as-text problem.

The clean table is now: three rows, one header, one value per cell, one spelling per region, and a SUM that matches the data. Each step is a decision a reader can re-trace against the raw export.

## Decision Rule

Use the audience first, the evidence second, and the tool third. A digital product is justified only when another person can see what changed, why it changed, and what evidence supports the change.

Use the following check when the artifact looks finished but the reasoning may still be hidden:

1. **Purpose check:** What should the audience be able to do after reading or using the artifact?
2. **Evidence check:** Which exact source, observation, annotation, rubric line, data pattern, or policy boundary justifies the revision?
3. **Revision check:** What changed in the artifact, and what suggestion did you reject or modify?

If you cannot answer all three questions, the artifact may be complete, but it is not yet ready as evidence of learning.
