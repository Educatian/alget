## Core Concept

The core idea in this section is that **Teacher Noticing and Revision** should be treated as a relationship among purpose, constraint, evidence, and revision. Purpose names what the learner or audience needs to accomplish. Constraint names what limits the decision. Evidence names the source that makes the decision defensible. Revision shows what changed after the evidence was considered.

Three course anchors shape the reasoning here:

- **TPACK** helps you decide what counts as a meaningful learning or performance demand.
- **critical edtech studies** helps you notice when a design or technology choice creates risk, burden, or unsupported assumptions.
- **AI and education datafication** helps you check whether the artifact works for varied learners, audiences, or use contexts.

Read the anchors as competing checks on quality. One may highlight the learning demand, another may expose a risk, and another may help you test transfer.

## Worked Example

Walk through one annotated judgment call that moves from noticing to revision. A teacher finishes a geometry lesson and reviews twenty exit tickets.

**Step 1. Record the observable, not the verdict.**

> **Weak note (rejected):** "The class just does not get geometry." That is a verdict with nothing to act on.

> **Noticing note (kept):** "Six of twenty exit tickets gave the perimeter when the question asked for area; four of those six wrote the correct area formula but then added the side lengths instead of multiplying." The teacher described what was on the page before deciding what it meant.

**Step 2. Weigh the moment against the pattern.** One confused student would warrant a quick check. Six tickets showing the same area-versus-perimeter swap is a pattern worth a revision, and the teacher notes that distinction so a single reaction does not trigger an overhaul.

**Step 3. Make the cause-linked revision.** Recorded on the map: "Because six tickets confused area with perimeter, tomorrow opens with a two-minute sort: students place six tasks under 'measures the inside' or 'measures the border' before any formula appears. Rejected option: reteach the area formula again, because the tickets show the formula is known; the two ideas are being confused, not the procedure."

**Step 4. Leave the link visible.** The map now ties the described observation directly to the revision and explains the rejected alternative. A mentor can trace why the change was made, not just that a change happened.

## Decision Rule

Use the classroom value conflict first, the technology affordance second, and the policy boundary third. A tool choice is justified only when it protects students while improving a specific learning activity.

A strong submission should survive three quick tests:

1. **Purpose check:** What should the audience be able to do after reading or using the artifact?
2. **Evidence check:** Which exact source, observation, annotation, rubric line, data pattern, or policy boundary justifies the revision?
3. **Revision check:** What changed in the artifact, and what suggestion did you reject or modify?

If you cannot answer all three questions, the artifact may be complete, but it is not yet ready as evidence of learning.
