# Transferring Online Cases to Field Placements

![Transferring Online Cases to Field Placements textbook visual](/course-art/deep-exemplars/cat531-supplement/07-01-transferring-online-cases-to-field-placeme.png)

## Why This Section Matters

An online case is a controlled environment. The roster is fictional, the devices are assumed, the time is generous, and the messy variables of a real room have been edited out so you can practice one idea cleanly. A field placement removes all of that protection at once. The deepfake-detection lesson that earned full marks online may collide with a half-cart of Chromebooks, three IEP accommodations, and a bell that rings in 43 minutes. Transferring a case is not copying it; it is identifying which features of the case were essential to the learning goal and which were artifacts of the simulation, then rebuilding the lesson around your actual constraints.

This section treats the field-placement transfer note as the document where that distinction becomes visible. A strong note shows that you can tell a portable instructional idea from a setting-specific assumption, and that you adjusted the second without abandoning the first.

## Learning Targets

- Separate the transferable instructional goal of an online case from the setting-specific assumptions baked into the simulation.
- Map a fictional case roster onto a real placement roster, including device ratios, accommodations, and home-access constraints.
- Document a bounded AI/software support move with the suggestions you accepted and the ones you rejected as invented facts.
- Write a transfer note a mentor teacher can audit: original case, real constraint, specific change, and evidence the change worked.

## Opening Case

A case study is a teaching argument with the friction removed. When the module said "a class of 30 with reliable home laptops," that detail was not neutral; it silently licensed every design choice that followed, including the take-home reflection and the whole-class debate format. The transfer question is which of those choices survive contact with your placement.

The danger is treating the grade you earned online as proof the lesson is ready to teach. The grade certifies that you reasoned well inside the simulation's assumptions. It says nothing about whether those assumptions hold in your Tuesday placement. The transfer note exists to surface the gap between the two settings before students pay for it.

## Core Concept

Transferring a case turns on one move: decomposing the original lesson into a transferable core and a set of enabling conditions. The transferable core is the learning goal and the reasoning sequence that serves it (in the deepfake case, having students argue from evidence about a manipulated artifact). The enabling conditions are the resources the simulation provided for free: one device per student, dependable home connectivity, no accommodations, a class size that fits a single discussion circle.

When you move to a placement, the core should stay fixed while the enabling conditions are renegotiated against reality. This is the practical content of the TPACK idea that technology, pedagogy, and content are interdependent: change the technology context (14 devices instead of 30) and the pedagogy must change with it (stations instead of whole-class), or the content goal silently degrades.

Three lenses keep the renegotiation honest:

- **TPACK** asks whether the lesson's technological demand still fits the learning goal once the device reality changes, or whether you are now teaching the tool instead of the content.
- **Critical edtech studies** asks who the original assumptions excluded. "Reliable home laptops" is not a neutral default; it encodes a class assumption that the take-home step quietly enforces.
- **AI and education datafication** asks what a generative tool will do when you ask it to adapt the case. It tends to restore the convenient assumptions (one-to-one devices, named cooperative students) because those make for a cleaner narrative.

The test of whether a lens did real work is simple: it should have changed a concrete feature of your lesson (a format, a grouping, a dropped step), not just appeared in your rationale.

## Worked Example

Compare two versions of a transfer note. The thin version copies the case forward and certifies it with the online grade:

> I will run the deepfake-detection debate from the module and assign the take-home reflection. It earned full marks online.

This is not a transfer note; it is a paste. It treats the simulation's enabling conditions as if they were facts about the placement. A note that actually does the work looks like this:

> The module assumed 30 students, one device each, and reliable home access. My placement has 14 Chromebooks for 28 students, three students with read-aloud and extended-time accommodations, and nine students who reported no reliable home device. I kept the evidence-based debate (the core) and changed the delivery: a three-station rotation so 14 devices serve 28, the take-home reflection dropped because nine students could not complete it, and read-aloud preloaded for the three accommodated students.

The second version is not longer for its own sake. It names the original assumption, the real constraint that breaks it, and the specific structural change that preserves the learning goal anyway.

### Annotated Judgment Call: Transferring the Deepfake-Detection Case

Work the move on a real scenario. Maya completed an online module case in which candidates debated a deepfake-detection lesson for a fictional class of 30 with reliable home laptops. She now plans to teach it in her Tuesday placement.

1. **Before (copy-over draft):** "I will run the deepfake-detection debate from the module and assign the take-home reflection. It earned full marks online."
2. **Observed constraint:** Her placement has one shared cart of 14 Chromebooks for 28 students, and 3 students have IEP accommodations for read-aloud and extended time. The fictional roster modeled none of this.
3. **After (transfer note):** "I converted the live whole-class debate into a 3-station rotation so 14 devices serve 28 students. I dropped the take-home step because 9 of 28 students reported no reliable home device. I preloaded the read-aloud setting for the 3 students with that accommodation."
4. **Suggestion rejected:** An AI rewrite invented one-to-one laptops and named imaginary students. I removed those invented facts because they contradicted what I observed.
5. **Evidence the change worked:** In the placement, 26 of 28 students completed the station task, versus the 9 who would have finished the take-home version based on the home-device count.

The judgment is visible: a named placement constraint drove a specific change, and the candidate can show what students did differently because of it.

## Decision Rule

When you transfer a case, resolve conflicts in this order: protect the learning goal first, satisfy the access constraint second, and preserve the original format only third. Most novice transfers invert this and protect the format ("it has to be a whole-class debate") at the expense of access. The deepfake case shows the correct order: the format (whole-class) was the most expendable element, the access constraint (14 devices, no home access) was non-negotiable, and the goal (argue from evidence about a manipulated artifact) was preserved by changing the format to fit the constraint.

Before you teach a transferred case, three checks should pass:

1. **Core check:** Can you state the one learning goal that must survive the move, separate from how the case happened to deliver it?
2. **Assumption check:** Which enabling conditions did the simulation provide that your placement does not, and which lesson step depends on each one?
3. **Change check:** What did you restructure to preserve the goal under your real constraints, and what AI-suggested "fix" did you reject because it reintroduced an assumption you do not have?

If any check fails, you have copied the case, not transferred it.

The most common error is reading the online grade as a clearance to teach. It is not; it certifies reasoning inside the simulation, not fit with your placement. A second error is "feature transfer," where the candidate preserves the surface format (the debate, the take-home) and lets the learning goal quietly erode because the format no longer fits the room. A third is trusting an AI adaptation that restores convenient assumptions; when a rewrite hands you one-to-one devices and cooperative named students, it has optimized for a tidy story, not your roster.

## Textbook Walkthrough

Apply the decomposition to a fresh case. Suppose an online module taught a collaborative-annotation lesson where 24 students mark up a shared document in real time, each on a laptop. The transferable core is collaborative close reading where students make their thinking visible to each other. The enabling conditions are one device per student and a platform the district has approved.

Now drop it into a placement with 12 tablets and a district that has not vetted the annotation tool. The core survives only if you change the conditions: pair students so 12 devices serve 24, and substitute a printed shared text with a document camera for the platform the district will not allow. If you instead keep the original platform because the case used it, you have let an enabling condition override the learning goal and the access reality at once. The walkthrough is the same every time: name the core, list what the simulation gave you for free, and rebuild only the conditions while leaving the core untouched.

## What Strong Reading Looks Like

As you read a case, keep a two-column note: **transferable core** on the left, **simulation assumption** on the right. Every detail in the case belongs in one column. "Argue from evidence about a manipulated image" is core. "Reliable home laptops" is assumption. The discipline of sorting forces you to notice the conditions the case smuggled in as if they were features.

A strong reader of a transferable case can answer four questions:

1. What single learning goal is this case really teaching, stripped of its delivery format?
2. Which enabling condition, if removed, would break the lesson the fastest?
3. Which condition encodes an equity assumption that excludes some of my actual students?
4. What would I rebuild if my device ratio, home-access reality, or accommodation profile changed again?

## Stronger Artifact Standard

A transfer note is strong when a mentor teacher, reading only the note, can reconstruct both the original case and your placement without you in the room. That means the note states the case's assumptions explicitly (not just its activities), names the specific constraint that breaks each assumption, and shows the structural change that preserved the goal. Polish (clean formatting, a tidy table) is the last layer; if the note reads smoothly but cannot tell the reader what the simulation assumed, it has skipped the layer that matters.

A useful closing paragraph sounds like this: "I changed the delivery of this case because the simulation assumed resources my placement does not have, and copying it forward would have failed the nine students without home devices. I rejected the AI rewrite that restored one-to-one laptops, because looking adaptable is not the same as being teachable in this room."

## Transfer Case

Take the same deepfake lesson to a different placement: a one-to-one laptop school, but with a strict district policy banning any tool that uploads student-created media to an outside server. Now the device constraint disappears and the take-home step becomes viable again, but a new enabling condition (free upload of media for analysis) breaks. The transferable core has not moved; the lesson still argues from evidence about a manipulated artifact. What changes is which condition you renegotiate. If you can re-derive the lesson under this different broken assumption rather than reusing your station rotation out of habit, you understand transfer rather than one specific fix.
