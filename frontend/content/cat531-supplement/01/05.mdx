## Core Concept

The core idea in this section is that **Classroom Context Variables** should be treated as a relationship among purpose, constraint, evidence, and revision. Purpose names what the learner or audience needs to accomplish. Constraint names what limits the decision. Evidence names the source that makes the decision defensible. Revision shows what changed after the evidence was considered.

Three course anchors shape the reasoning here:

- **TPACK** helps you decide what counts as a meaningful learning or performance demand.
- **critical edtech studies** helps you notice when a design or technology choice creates risk, burden, or unsupported assumptions.
- **AI and education datafication** helps you check whether the artifact works for varied learners, audiences, or use contexts.

Use these anchors as lenses, not as names to drop into a reflection. A useful lens changes what you notice, what you question, and what you revise.

## Worked Example

Scenario: A teacher reads that a discussion-bot "raised participation by 30 percent in a published study" and wants to adopt it. The judgment turns on whether her classroom context matches the study's. Walk it in four numbered moves.

**Before (context-blind framing).** Her note reads:

> The study shows the discussion bot raises participation 30 percent, so I will roll it out to all my sections next week.

1. **Pull the study's context variables.** Reported setting: 12 students per class, 1:1 laptops, 0 multilingual learners flagged, all students with home broadband.
2. **Pull her own context variables.** Her setting: 34 students, 10 shared tablets on a cart, 9 multilingual learners, 7 students with IEP/504 accommodations, about a third without reliable home internet.
3. **Compare variable by variable.** Class size 12 vs. 34. Device ratio 1:1 vs. roughly 1:3.4. Language profile 0 vs. 9 multilingual. The bot's text-only, fast-typing interaction model fits the study's room but not hers.
4. **Decide using the mismatch.** The 30 percent figure does not transfer. She will pilot the bot with one 34-student section, on a rotation so the device ratio is manageable, with a voice-input fallback for multilingual learners, and measure participation in HER room before any wider rollout.

**After (context-aware framing).** The revised note reads:

> The 30 percent study ran with 12 students, 1:1 devices, and no multilingual learners. My section has 34 students, a 1:3.4 device ratio, and 9 multilingual learners. I am not assuming the result transfers. I will pilot one section with device rotation and a voice-input fallback, then check my own participation data before scaling.

The decision is defensible because the context variables that govern transfer were listed and compared, so the borrowed evidence is tested against this room rather than assumed to apply.

## Decision Rule

Use the classroom value conflict first, the technology affordance second, and the policy boundary third. A tool choice is justified only when it protects students while improving a specific learning activity.

Before submitting the Design Tension Studio map naming a classroom value conflict and negotiated decision, slow down and check whether the trace can answer three questions:

1. **Purpose check:** What should the audience be able to do after reading or using the artifact?
2. **Evidence check:** Which exact source, observation, annotation, rubric line, data pattern, or policy boundary justifies the revision?
3. **Revision check:** What changed in the artifact, and what suggestion did you reject or modify?

If you cannot answer all three questions, the artifact may be complete, but it is not yet ready as evidence of learning.

## Common Misreadings

A common mistake is to defend a technology because it is engaging. Engagement matters, but it does not replace evidence about learning, access, or risk.

The artifact should show how student need, evidence, and policy boundaries shaped the revision.

The evidence is the exact phrase, example, annotation, support move, or artifact revision that makes the claim inspectable.
