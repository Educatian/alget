## Why This Section Matters

When a tool is mandated, it is tempting to believe the decisions have all been made elsewhere and your only options are compliance or quiet sabotage. That belief is both false and consequential, because a mandated platform still has to land in a real room with real students, and how it lands is almost always within your control. Agency is not the freedom to refuse the mandate; it is the authority and the responsibility for the part of the decision that remains yours.

This section matters because the most common failures with mandated technology are failures of abdication, not defiance. A teacher who runs the default sequence unchanged has not "followed policy"; she has handed the sequencing of student learning to the platform's logic and stopped being the professional in the loop. Teacher agency is the disciplined practice of adapting and justifying deviations within a constraint, documenting them, and feeding evidence back into the policy so the constraint itself can improve.

## Opening Case

A district mandates an adaptive math platform that auto-assigns each student a next lesson. A teacher notices it routes her struggling readers past a fractions-vocabulary step they need, but the platform is required, so her first instinct is to let it assign whatever it assigns and treat any struggle as the platform's design, not her call. That instinct quietly surrenders the one decision that was still hers: how the mandate lands for these specific students.

Professional judgment becomes visible the moment she stops asking "am I allowed to change this?" and starts asking "where, inside this mandate, do I still have authority, and what does the evidence tell me to do with it?"

## Core Concept

Teacher agency with technology is the capacity to make and justify decisions within a constraint, rather than the freedom to remove the constraint. It lives in the gap between blind compliance (running the default and disowning the result) and covert refusal (ignoring the mandate without record). Both extremes give up agency; one to the platform, the other to the shadows.

Exercising agency well has a recognizable structure:

- **Locate the surrendered decision.** Identify what you handed to the tool by accepting its default. Sequencing, pacing, grouping, what counts as "done." Naming it is the precondition for reclaiming it.
- **Find the specific harm with evidence.** Agency without evidence is just preference reasserting itself. Tie the deviation to a measured effect on named students.
- **Act within the bounds.** Make the smallest justified adaptation that addresses the harm while honoring the mandate, and log the deviation.
- **Feed evidence upward.** Bring the data to whoever owns the policy so the constraint can be revised. Agency includes acting on the policy, not only inside it.

Documentation is what separates agency from refusal: a logged, evidence-backed deviation is a professional act a colleague can inspect, while an unlogged one is indistinguishable from non-compliance. This is agency in the ecological sense Biesta, Priestley and Robinson (2015) describe: something achieved within institutional and structural constraints, not a free-floating capacity to refuse them. Critical edtech studies names the harm a default can cause; [TPACK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_pedagogical_content_knowledge) keeps the adaptation tied to the content goal; datafication research (Perrotta & Selwyn, 2020) reminds you that the platform's routing logic is itself a designed artifact, not a fact of nature.

## Worked Example

Scenario: the teacher from the opening case decides what exercising agency looks like inside the math-platform mandate. Walk it in four numbered moves.

**Before (compliance-only framing).** Her note reads:

> The platform is required, so I let it assign whatever it assigns. If students struggle, that is the platform's design, not my call.

1. **Locate the lost agency.** By running the default sequence unchanged, she has handed the sequencing decision to the platform's logic, including the skipped vocabulary step.
2. **Find the specific harm with evidence.** Over two weeks, the 6 students the platform fast-tracked scored 40 percent on the fractions-vocabulary quiz, versus 80 percent for the rest of the class.
3. **Exercise bounded agency within the mandate.** She keeps the platform as required but inserts a 10-minute vocabulary mini-lesson before the auto-assigned task for those 6 students, and logs the change.
4. **Feed evidence back into the policy.** She brings the 40-vs-80 gap to her grade team to request that the platform's prerequisite-skip setting be disabled district-wide.

**After (agency-within-constraint framing).** The revised note reads:

> I use the mandated platform but I am the author of how it lands. Evidence: it skipped a fractions-vocabulary step and the 6 fast-tracked students scored 40 percent vs. 80 percent for the class. I inserted a 10-minute vocabulary step for those students and submitted the gap data to my team to get the skip setting changed. I documented every deviation.

The decision is defensible because agency is shown as the capacity to adapt and justify deviations within the mandate, backed by evidence, rather than as either blind compliance or covert refusal.

Work the mandate as a live dilemma. Steer between blind compliance and covert refusal: reclaim the decision the default made, back it with evidence, and push it upward.

## Decision Rule

Inside a mandate, name the value the default is harming first, weigh the affordance second, and confirm the policy boundary third so your deviation stays within bounds. A tool choice, or a tool adaptation, is justified only when it protects students while improving a specific learning activity, and the deviation is documented.

A defensible exercise of agency survives three quick tests:

1. **Purpose check:** What decision did the default quietly make for you, and is reclaiming it in service of a learning goal?
2. **Evidence check:** What measured harm to named students justifies the deviation, rather than a hunch?
3. **Revision check:** Did you log the deviation and route the evidence to whoever can change the policy?

**Privacy note before routing data upward.** A named-student deviation log and routing data are education records under FERPA, and for students under 13 the vendor side is governed by COPPA, whose amended rule was finalized in 2025 with compliance dates in 2026. Between the finalized COPPA amendments and the wave of state student-data-privacy and school-AI guidance, check your district's current policy before exporting named-student gap data beyond the building.

If the deviation is unlogged or unevidenced, it is refusal, not agency.

## Common Misreadings

The first error is equating agency with refusal, as if the only autonomous act is to ignore the mandate; this forfeits documentation and the chance to change the policy. The second is the mirror image, reading a mandate as total, so that any struggle becomes "the platform's fault"; this surrenders the decisions that genuinely remained yours. A third is exercising agency on intuition alone; a deviation with no evidence is not professional judgment, it is preference wearing a lab coat, and a colleague cannot defend it for you.

## Textbook Walkthrough

Apply the structure to a new mandate: a school requires all feedback to go through an AI comment-bank that inserts canned praise and correction phrases. A teacher feels the comments miss her students' actual reasoning.

Locate the surrendered decision. By accepting the default phrases, she has handed the substance of feedback, what students hear about their thinking, to a generic bank. That is the authority to reclaim.

Find the harm with evidence. She reviews a week of bot comments on her 5 advanced writers and finds the canned phrases praised surface neatness while ignoring the argument flaws she had flagged in class; two students then doubled down on weak claims, citing the bot's praise.

Act within bounds and feed upward. She keeps the comment-bank for routine mechanics but appends a personal note on argument quality for essays where the bank misses the point, logging each override. She brings the two-student example to her department to request that the bank allow teacher-authored comments to take precedence. The walkthrough shows the same agency structure on a feedback mandate: name what the default decided, evidence the harm, deviate within bounds with a record, and push the evidence toward the policy.

## What Strong Reading Looks Like

As you read, keep a two-column note: the **decision the default made for the teacher** on the left, the **evidence and bounded deviation that reclaim it** on the right. For this topic, the tell that you are reading well is that you can distinguish a logged, evidenced deviation from an unlogged one and explain why only the first counts as agency.

A strong reader of teacher agency can answer four questions:

1. What decision did accepting the default hand to the tool?
2. What measured harm to which students justifies reclaiming it?
3. What is the smallest deviation that addresses the harm while honoring the mandate?
4. How was the deviation documented and routed back to the policy owner?

## Stronger Artifact Standard

The artifact is strong when it shows a reclaimed decision, the evidence of harm that justified it, a bounded and logged deviation, and a path that carries the evidence back to the policy. An artifact that only complies, or only refuses, has skipped the agency entirely; mark that gap and ask BigAL to compare a "ran the default" note against a "documented bounded deviation" note rather than for a general explanation.

A useful closing line sounds like: "I use the mandated platform but author how it lands; the 40-vs-80 gap justified a 10-minute vocabulary step for 6 students, logged, with the gap data sent to my team to change the skip setting."

## Transfer Case

Move the agency question into a setting with no mandate at all, where the teacher freely chose the tool. The structure inverts: there is no default someone else set, so the surrendered-decision step disappears, but the evidence and documentation steps remain, now aimed at justifying her own free choice to a reviewer. If your reasoning only works against an external mandate, you learned to push back on authority; if it also disciplines your own free choices, you understood agency as accountable authorship.
