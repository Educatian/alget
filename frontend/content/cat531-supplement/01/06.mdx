## Core Concept

The core idea in this section is that **Teacher Agency With Technology** should be treated as a relationship among purpose, constraint, evidence, and revision. Purpose names what the learner or audience needs to accomplish. Constraint names what limits the decision. Evidence names the source that makes the decision defensible. Revision shows what changed after the evidence was considered.

Three course anchors shape the reasoning here:

- **TPACK** helps you decide what counts as a meaningful learning or performance demand.
- **critical edtech studies** helps you notice when a design or technology choice creates risk, burden, or unsupported assumptions.
- **AI and education datafication** helps you check whether the artifact works for varied learners, audiences, or use contexts.

The anchors should do work in your artifact. If TPACK or critical edtech studies does not change a decision, it is only a citation rather than evidence of judgment.

## Worked Example

Scenario: A district mandates an adaptive math platform that auto-assigns each student a next lesson. A teacher notices it routes her struggling readers past a fractions-vocabulary step they need. The question is what exercising agency looks like inside a mandate. Walk it in four numbered moves.

**Before (compliance-only framing).** Her note reads:

> The platform is required, so I let it assign whatever it assigns. If students struggle, that is the platform's design, not my call.

1. **Locate the lost agency.** By running the default sequence unchanged, she has handed the sequencing decision to the platform's logic, including the skipped vocabulary step.
2. **Find the specific harm with evidence.** Over two weeks, the 6 students the platform fast-tracked scored 40 percent on the fractions-vocabulary quiz, versus 80 percent for the rest of the class.
3. **Exercise bounded agency within the mandate.** She keeps the platform as required but inserts a 10-minute vocabulary mini-lesson before the auto-assigned task for those 6 students, and logs the change.
4. **Feed evidence back into the policy.** She brings the 40-vs-80 gap to her grade team to request that the platform's prerequisite-skip setting be disabled district-wide.

**After (agency-within-constraint framing).** The revised note reads:

> I use the mandated platform but I am the author of how it lands. Evidence: it skipped a fractions-vocabulary step and the 6 fast-tracked students scored 40 percent vs. 80 percent for the class. I inserted a 10-minute vocabulary step for those students and submitted the gap data to my team to get the skip setting changed. I documented every deviation.

The decision is defensible because agency is shown as the capacity to adapt and justify deviations within the mandate, backed by evidence, rather than as either blind compliance or covert refusal.

## Decision Rule

Use the classroom value conflict first, the technology affordance second, and the policy boundary third. A tool choice is justified only when it protects students while improving a specific learning activity.

Use the following check when the artifact looks finished but the reasoning may still be hidden:

1. **Purpose check:** What should the audience be able to do after reading or using the artifact?
2. **Evidence check:** Which exact source, observation, annotation, rubric line, data pattern, or policy boundary justifies the revision?
3. **Revision check:** What changed in the artifact, and what suggestion did you reject or modify?

If you cannot answer all three questions, the artifact may be complete, but it is not yet ready as evidence of learning.

## Common Misreadings

Another misreading is to write a general teaching reflection after the artifact is finished instead of documenting the decision while it is being revised.

A colleague should be able to see why the tool belongs in this learning activity and where its limits remain.
