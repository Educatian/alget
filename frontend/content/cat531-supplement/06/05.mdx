## Core Concept

The core idea in this section is that **Risk Mitigation and Family Communication** should be treated as a relationship among purpose, constraint, evidence, and revision. Purpose names what the learner or audience needs to accomplish. Constraint names what limits the decision. Evidence names the source that makes the decision defensible. Revision shows what changed after the evidence was considered.

Three course anchors shape the reasoning here:

- **TPACK** helps you decide what counts as a meaningful learning or performance demand.
- **critical edtech studies** helps you notice when a design or technology choice creates risk, burden, or unsupported assumptions.
- **AI and education datafication** helps you check whether the artifact works for varied learners, audiences, or use contexts.

Read the anchors as competing checks on quality. One may highlight the learning demand, another may expose a risk, and another may help you test transfer.

### Worked Example: An Annotated Family-Communication Decision

Follow a teacher revising a family letter and the underlying AI-policy memo for a new AI tutoring tool.

1. **Before (reassurance).** "We are excited to use an innovative, FERPA-aware AI tool this year." A reviewer annotates: "This reassures but discloses nothing a family can act on."
2. **Disclose specifics.** Revision states: "Your child's essays are sent to the AI tutor for feedback. Submissions are stored 30 days and are not used to train the model."
3. **Make the opt-out real.** Draft opt-out routed every family through the same upload. Fix: opting out gives teacher-only feedback with no upload, a genuinely different path that avoids the declined risk.
4. **Verify the claims.** The retention figure came from a BigAL draft. The teacher checks the tool's data agreement and corrects "indefinite" to the verified "30 days" before sending.
5. **Make it accessible (after).** Reissue the letter at an 8th-grade reading level with a Spanish translation, because consent from a notice families cannot read is not informed consent.

The before reassures; the after discloses specific data handling, offers a real alternative, verifies the facts, and reaches every family.

## Decision Rule

Use the classroom value conflict first, the technology affordance second, and the policy boundary third. A tool choice is justified only when it protects students while improving a specific learning activity.

A strong submission should survive three quick tests:

1. **Purpose check:** What should the audience be able to do after reading or using the artifact?
2. **Evidence check:** Which exact source, observation, annotation, rubric line, data pattern, or policy boundary justifies the revision?
3. **Revision check:** What changed in the artifact, and what suggestion did you reject or modify?

If you cannot answer all three questions, the artifact may be complete, but it is not yet ready as evidence of learning.
